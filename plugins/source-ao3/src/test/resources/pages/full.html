We're Not Saints

Anonymous

Summary:

Ling Yao has been stuck in a basement for days with no contact from the outside world. It's not an ideal situation and he knows no one is going to come for him.

He's confident Edward Elric will make sure of that.

Notes:

Heed the tags. This is not a light hearted story.

Chapter 1: Argument

Chapter Text

Ling sat in a chair, hands bound behind his back, ankles tied to the legs of the chair, and a cloth gag in his mouth.

He'd been sitting in a dingy, cold room. It was presumably a basement. He wasn't sure. He wasn't awake when he was brought here. There was a sense of panic, at first, having woken up in this empty room. The only source of light was from under the slight crack under the door. The lightbulb that hung above his head ran out two days after his arrival.

His captor only came in once. Unlike most kidnappers, he didn't bother trying to hide his identity, not wearing a mask or cover himself up in any way. It was a man Ling didn't recognize, but that wasn't anything new. There were too many people in this field who committed various crimes. It was rare to come across the same person.

The man didn't say something Ling hadn't heard before. Typical threats, taunting Ling for being in this situation, telling him the only way he was going to get out was if ransom was paid. If there wasn't payment, the man threatened to take him to a market and sell him. Healthy teenagers sold for a lot, he was told.

Little did the kidnapper know that Ling had been sold before.

It was easy to tune him out eventually. Ling tried to focus on the pain on the back of his head instead. The hit to his head was not strong enough to kill him, his heart hadn't stopped, but it was enough to keep him out for at least half a day. That was all he could figure out on his own when he woke up.

When the man was done his spiel, telling Ling how much money he was going to put on his ransom, he left.

What the kidnapper didn't know was that no one was going to pay for ransom. Even if it was something as little as $100. No one was going to help him. The fight he had with Edward Elric made him completely sure of that.

Now, Ling had to wait and wonder how long it was going to take the kidnapper to realize that. How long was he going to have to wait to be sold to the highest bidder?

Ling sighed through his nose, closing his eyes. His mind wandered off to five days ago when he was taken.

When he had his fight with Edward.

It took Ling three hours to revive from his recent death at the drug dealer's building. It took him another two hours to get back to the office, where he knew Edward was going to be, more likely than not writing up some report Mustang wanted him to do.

Whenever Ling died, he preferred to take an hour long nap or sometimes more than an hour. It helped him to get all his energy back, as if he hadn't died at all, and was simply knocked out for a few hours. Moving too soon exhausted him.

There were times he had no choice but to move soon, depending on the situation. Tonight was one of those situations. When he woke up, Edward wasn't around, nor was the drug dealer. Ling figured Edward took care of him himself or called backup from their unit. He really wished Edward had stuck around so Ling knew how he was holding up. Most of the time, if Ling died in front of Edward, he waited for Ling to come around. This was one of the only times he didn't, and it worried Ling.

Ling didn't have much time to clean himself up as he wanted to get back to the office as soon as possible. He did manage to clean all the blood off his face and head before entering the office, however. He ignored the greetings from his fellow teammates, making a beeline for Edward's desk. He had, in fact, been filling out a report.

"What happened, Edward?" Ling asked. "Are you okay?"

Edward didn't look at him.

"Why didn't you stay around, after…you know."

"I was busy."

Ling sighed. If he was worried before, he was more so now with how clipped Edward's response was.

"So you got the dealer, then?"

"Yup."

That was good, at least. Because he left the scene, Ling assumed Edward dragged his body out of sight so their teammates wouldn't find his corpse. The only people who knew about Ling's immortality was Edward and the doctors who experimented on him three years ago. He'd prefer it to stay that way.

"So, uh…what did you tell the others? About why I wasn't there."

"Said you had to make an emergency run to the hotel because you were almost late on payment. Fell for it."

It was a good enough excuse. Ling was incredibly lucky he got the room he had at the hotel but he had to pay for it the exact day money was due or else he'd be kicked out. The unit had seen Ling worry about this before and let him off the hook if he had to make a call or run to the hotel and give payment.

"And what did the dealer say about what I did? I know he saw it. What did you tell the team about it?"

"Guy was high as fuck. It wasn't hard to write off why he said he saw you do what you did. I made sure the mess you made was cleaned up before calling the team. No one suspects anything."

Very good and clever on Edward's part. Ling felt guilty and bad that Edward had to clean up the mess, though.

None of this explained why Edward didn't stick around after the dealer was taken care of. He could have let the unit take the dealer on their own and wait for Ling to come around, like he usually did. Now, Edward wasn't looking at him at all and Ling couldn't think of a reason why he was so upset.

"Can we talk outside?"

Edward rolled his eyes. "Fine."

He pushed away the paperwork he was writing and stood from his desk, brushing past Ling. Ling followed after him. He didn't miss the looks the others were giving him and Edward but didn't comment on it.

They didn't talk until they were a few feet away from the office and further up the sidewalk. The whole time, Edward had his hands shoved his his pant pockets, staring at the ground. He stopped and pressed his back against a building, scowl ever present.

"Why are you so upset, Ed?" Ling asked.

"Why do you think?" Edward said.

"I don't know. That's why I'm asking."

Edward didn't say anything for a few long moments to the point Ling was wondering if he was going to answer him at all. He was thinking of another way to get an answer out of him but didn't get the chance. Edward's golden eyes shot up, glaring holes into Ling. Ling stepped back.

"You said you'd stop dying in front of me, and what do you do? You shoot yourself in the fucking head!"

Oh, that.

Ling supposed it made sense why that would be upsetting to watch. To most, it'd appear as if he was committing suicide. Had he been a normal person, he'd be dead for good. Maybe that was what Edward was thinking about when he put his own gun to his head. But Edward knew he wasn't normal. His reaction was confusing.

"I don't understand why you're so upset. You know I revive within a few hours."

"You know what, fuck you."

Edward turned on his heel and stalked back toward the office. Ling didn't follow him, watching his partner leave him alone with his thoughts.

It wasn't fair. Edward didn't give him a chance to try and explain why he shot himself in the head. It might have been scary to witness, but Ling had a sure fire plan. It wasn't one he could have shared with Edward though, because he knew Edward wouldn't approve of it and they didn't have time to go back and forth arguing about it.

Shooting himself in the head was meant to be a distraction while confronting the dealer they were after. Ling was going to shoot himself to get the criminal's attention, then that would give Edward the chance to tackle him and put him in cuffs.

And Edward confirmed to him that it worked.

Ling sighed. Edward knew he'd come back in a couple hours, so he had plenty of time to calm down and remember that fact, to reassure himself Ling was going to see him again.

Why was this time any different compared to before?

There was always the possibility Edward was thinking of his younger brother, Alphonse. How he wished to see him and that he could come back from the dead. Alphonse was a subject they didn't touch often, if at all. Ling only knew the bare minimum and that was enough for him.

He still didn't think it was fair for Edward to lose his temper on him, no matter what it was Edward was thinking about when Ling shot himself.

Ling had no reason to go back to the office. Edward was safe, the dealer was taken care of, and he was back from the dead. All in a day's work. There was nothing else that needed to be done. Besides, Ling didn't want to deal with Edward's mood anymore today.

They could talk tomorrow after they had more time to themselves.

It was two hours later, after the argument, when Ling was attacked. If it could even be called an attack. It was rather one sided.

Ling had been walking back to his hotel, looking at his phone to see if there were any PSAs from Roy Mustang, when something hard and heavy hit his back. He stumbled and collapsed to the ground, phone falling out of his grip. He immediately tried reaching for the device, ready to call Mustang as he was his emergency contact for such situations.

The object, a metal pipe, Ling learned, hit the phone, rendering it useless.

Despite how sore his back was, Ling attempted to get up to run away. He didn't have any specific destination in mind. He just needed to get moving.

He barely made it to his knees when the pipe slammed against the back of his head.

It was a miracle it hadn't killed him.

Ling's eyes shot open when the door behind him slammed open. He shifted the best he could in his chair, trying to catch a glimpse of what was happening. The small bit of hope he had in his chest dwindled when he saw it was only his captor who barged in, not someone from his unit.

He shouldn't have been surprised or had even the smallest bit of hope. Edward was making sure no one was coming for him.

The man walked in front of Ling, all the while Ling kept his eyes on him. He thought the man had a lot of nerve to look frustrated when he wasn't the one who had been bound and gagged for the past five days.

"You aren't very loved, are you?" the man said.

Straight to the point. Unfortunately for his kidnapper, Ling didn't feel hurt. He shrugged.

"I'm going to give it one more day. If no one pays for your ransom by then, we're going for a little ride."

Wonderful. So Ling had to wait another day until he's out of this damn room. It'd be much better to be let out now. One more day won't make a difference. They should just get this over with now and make it easier for both of them.

Especially because Ling's hands and feet were becoming numb. As a matter of fact, he could barely feel them anymore. The zip ties weren't bad at first but now they're taking their toll and it freaking hurt. He was not looking forward to the pins and needles that were going to come once he was untied. He'd also like the gag out of his mouth so he can talk.

Ling tried to show he wanted (and needed) his hands and feet loose by trying to pull them out of their bindings. His captor didn't seem to get the message or didn't care because all he did was look at him with a bored expression.

"You're not getting out."

Obviously. It wasn't his fault the guy wasn't smart enough to realize what Ling was trying to ask for. Ling groaned, stopped moving, and shifted his gaze to the side. Not being able to speak was almost as annoying as not being able to move his hands and feet.

The man mockingly patted the top of his head and finally left Ling alone.

One more day.

Then he'll be able to get somewhere.

Chapter 2: Nothing

Chapter Text

It was 2:00PM when Edward pushed his way into Mustang's office. He just finished doing another sweep around Ling's hotel neighbourhood with Fuery. Today was another failure. Not even Black Hayate picked up any new scents or trails.

"Mustang, you got anything yet?"

Mustang didn't answer right away, fingers typing on his keyboard, a concentrated frown on his face. Edward collapsed into one of the chairs in front of his superior's desk. He was tempted to kick his feet up on the desk to get Mustang to look at him but decided against it.

After two agonizing minutes of nothing but the sound of typing, Mustang pulled his hands back and folded them on his desk, looking at Edward.

"I'm still working on it," the former Colonel said. "If I did have something, I would have called you right away. I told you that from the start."

Edward crossed his arms. "Yeah, well. You could always forget. I want news as soon as possible."

"And when I get some, I'll share it. Until then, you need to let me do my job."

Edward looked out the big window behind Mustang's desk, eyes roaming around the main floor of the office until they landed on Ling's empty desk. He didn't expect Mustang to understand why he wanted Ling back as badly as he did. Mustang no doubt thought it was because he was thinking of his missing, presumably dead, brother. How he lost him three years ago and wasn't going to get him back. He probably also thought Edward only wanted Ling back because they're best friends.

In theory, all of that was true to some degree. None of that was the main reason, though. He wanted- needed Ling back so he could apologize to him. He didn't want his last words to Ling be 'fuck you'.

Just because his friend was immortal didn't mean he couldn't be tortured. It didn't mean he didn't feel pain. It didn't mean they'd find him. Ling could be gone forever, just like Alphonse.

He shouldn't have been so angry that day. He should have let Ling explain himself and why he did what he did. Would he still be upset? Probably. Edward never liked watching Ling die, even with the knowledge that he would come back within a few hours. It was disturbing. When people died, they weren't supposed to come back to life. They were supposed to remain dead and get a funeral.

Ling said that being immortal was a blessing and a curse. Edward argued it was more of a curse.

"Listen to me, Edward. Ling went missing recently, so there is a high chance we're going to find him."

'Unlike Alphonse' didn't need to be said but Edward knew that was what Mustang was thinking. Hell, it was a thought that kept popping up in his head too. He wasn't sure how to feel about that.

"Alive," Mustang added, as if that was supposed to make a difference.

"I'm not worried about that," Edward said. He glanced at Mustang and quickly made something up on the spot when he saw the confusion etched on Mustang's face. "He's a stubborn bastard. I'm sure he'll live."

That was the closest Edward could get to saying Ling was immortal.

"Is there something about the night he went missing you haven't told me?" Mustang asked.

"No. We had a fight, that was it."

When Edward was first questioned by Mustang and got similar questions from the unit, Edward snapped. They had no right to accuse him of being responsible for Ling's disappearance. That wasn't why he felt guilty. He knew he wasn't the reason Ling disappeared.

Deep down, Edward knew the unit didn't actually blame him. They had to ask the questions. They needed as much information as they could get. He still couldn't help but feel like they were accusing him at the time. He settled down from that now and no one questioned him anymore.

"Wanna know what I wanna know?" Edward said, changing the subject. "Why hasn't there been a ransom? You'd think the night he disappeared someone would throw one out. 'Pay this much and we'll give him back', blah, blah, blah."

Mustang nodded in agreement. "I find that odd as well. The person who kidnapped him might not know the correct people to send the message to. Ling doesn't have anyone except for us and he has no reason to tell anyone that."

Edward mumbled, "Unless they torture it out of him."

"Clearly they didn't or else we would've gotten a ransom message. As things are now, there's nothing. Not even on websites regarding missing children. I haven't come across anything similar to a ransom letter."

Edward groaned loudly, tilting his head back and glaring at the ceiling. Everyone in the unit knew that Ling hadn't run away, even if he did have a fight with Edward. The only way Ling could disappear like this was someone taking him. That was the conclusion everyone came to day one.

They were lucky that they found his phone. It wasn't much to go on and it was broken but it was better than nothing. From where they found the phone it showed them Ling was heading back to his hotel when he was taken. How many hours it had been, nobody knew. The unit did an extensive search the first night and asked as many questions as possible to bystanders. It didn't get them anywhere. No one saw anything and there were no security cameras to look through.

All they needed was one email, one ransom letter, one phone call, some form of contact. That way, the unit could lock in and locate where the message came from. That might be another reason why there wasn't a message yet, because the kidnapper knew the unit could track them down instead of giving them the money. Although, Edward knew that if they had to, they would pay the ransom. Anything to get one of their teammates back.

No such luck.

"Why don't you go home and get some rest?"

Edward shook his head. "It's two in the afternoon. There's plenty of time to keep searching. I don't wanna sleep."

Every time he slept now, his mind conjured up horrible nightmares regarding Ling and his fate. Because his mind was doubly cruel, it also threw Alphonse in the nightmares as well. Edward had to watch two very important people in his life suffer. It didn't matter if it was in his head, it was far too real for his liking whenever he fell asleep.

"How about you try finding something to do other than search?" Mustang suggested. "You're going to drive yourself crazy at this rate."

"Mustang-"

"Edward." Mustang raised a hand to stop him. "Leave this to the adults. Everyone in the unit knows to contact you if there's news."

"I don't care. I want to be part of this. I owe it to Ling. He's my best friend."

"And your best friend wouldn't want you to burn out."

It took all Edward's willpower not to flip off Mustang, because what the hell did he know? Nothing. He stood from the chair and gave Mustang one last frustrated glare.

He had nothing more to say, so he left the office.

What was he supposed to do now?

There were no clues. There were no witnesses. Aside from finding Ling's phone, there wasn't any trace of him.

It really was like he had just disappeared into thin air.

Edward was starting to run out of ideas.

Chapter 3: Merchandise

Chapter Text

One arm tucked under his knees, the other his back. Ling was lifted carefully from his chair, body being shifted in a position where he was pressed against the chest of someone. He was still coming out of his sleepy daze, not having bothered to open his eyes yet. He didn't see a reason to, because he was safe now. Someone, presumably Mustang considering he was being carried, found him and was taking him away from the dingy room.

There was minor disappointment when he didn't hear Edward's voice. Mustang didn't speak either, but Edward was usually loud. If he wasn't talking, it meant he was still angry with him. Unless Mustang found him on his own and Edward wasn't here at all.

That thought had no right to hurt as much as it did.

Ling went to yawn to wake himself up a bit more, but paused when he felt something odd in his mouth. His hands and feet also felt off.

No. No, no, no…

Ling began squirming in the arms that had lifted him from that godforsaken chair. The grip on him tightened. When he opened his eyes, he found that he couldn't see anything. He didn't know if it was because of a blindfold or if there was a bag over his head.

He tried shouting. The gag in his mouth muffled his voice.

All he could tell was that he was being moved up stairs, and before long, warm air hit his body. It was overwhelming at first. It was such a stark contrast compared to the cold room he was trapped in for the past six days. His kidnapper didn't seem fazed by his attempts to get away and scream. They had to be in an alleyway then.

He didn't know what to do other than keep trying to get out of the kidnapper's grip and scream. Alleyway or not, somebody had to be around. Somebody had to walk close to the entrance of the alleyway eventually.

"Stop moving," his captor said.

Ling imagined him rolling his eyes.

"I told you, you had one more day. Now it's time to go to the market."

But Ling didn't want to be taken to the market. He didn't want to be sold again. Once was bad enough. Going through that again was worse than the experiments he was forced to go through three years ago.

Ling was thrown into what he assumed was the back of a truck or van, if the metalic floor was anything to go off of. He shuddered as the door slammed shut, causing the floor to vibrate momentarily. What was he supposed to do now? He wished he could cover his ears when the vehicle's engine loudly started. He wished he had something to hold onto when the vehicle jerked forward, making Ling's body slam back against a wall.

Damn, that hurt.

With great effort, Ling managed to pull himself up onto his knees. It brought minimal comfort to be in a less vulnerable position. His hands and feet were beginning to suffer from pins and needles and he tried desperately to force the zip ties off. Alas, he failed. He tried to work his jaw to do something about the gag but it must have been tied tighter because not even that seemed to shift.

The vehicle sped up and it was all Ling could do to remain sitting on his knees. A small part of him told him to try standing altogether and go to the door and try to kick it open. He knew he wouldn't be able to stand, though, considering the way he was tied.

There had to be some way out of this. Did he have to wait until they were at the market? He didn't know how far the market was. It could be hours away. It probably wasn't even in this city. The idea of leaving the city, being brought to who knows where, filled Ling with dread.

This was his fault, wasn't it?

Instead of leaving the office, he should have gone back inside with Edward, even if he was upset with him. It wasn't like they had to be close to each other. They could make some space and give each other time. But no. Ling had to leave. He just had to be looking at his phone instead of paying attention to his surroundings.

Ling slammed the back of his head against the cold wall.

Edward was right to be angry with him. He was a reckless fool.

He didn't blame Edward for not wanting to find him.

He didn't want to be sold again, but maybe he deserved it.

Ling had nothing better to do except pass out during the ride to the market, so that was what he did. He curled in on himself and slept, tried to ward off the nightmares that plagued him. It was only when the vehicle stopped that Ling realized he was starving and thirsty. Had he died from thirst or hunger yet? It was hard to tell. The one time he did was years ago and he didn't even notice because it only took him an hour to revive from it.

Maybe he died on the way to the market just to be revived in time. Wonderful.

The door opened. Ling's hearing picked up the sound of two individuals coming in. One grabbed his legs, the other moved behind him and picked him up by his shoulders. Ling was too tired to try kicking free, to pay attention to how awkward his position was.

"Wait until you see his face." That was his original captor. "He looks great for his age."

"Good. It's those ones that we want to sell."

If it was possible, Ling would have thrown up. Too bad it wasn't.

"He's also very healthy. He'll be able to do good work."

"He better be able to work."

Ling swallowed hard. His heartbeat was obnoxiously loud in his ears. He grunted when the men jumped out of the van, jerking uncomfortably in their grip. Couldn't they be more careful? He was merchandise for god's sake. Treat him with some decency.

His captors carried him for five minutes, talking amongst themselves as if Ling wasn't there, bringing up prices, what time he should be showcased, if he should be on the stage with others. It was tedious talk and while it was about him, he was too exhausted from all of this to care. He wanted this to be over with.

He was tossed to the ground. His hands brushed against dirt. There were no other voices except for the two men. The area smelled like he was in a stable or something resembling a stable.

The bag was ripped off Ling's head. He was immediately blinded by yellow overhead lights. He squinted and looked around him. His suspicions were confirmed; this was a stable. This might just be the preparation area until he was cleaned up and brought somewhere nicer.

Ling looked up. Just like before, neither men hid their faces and Ling didn't recognize the new one. That didn't mean anything anymore. His life was all but screwed.

"Told you he looked nice and healthy."

The new man leaned down, grabbed Ling's chin, and moved his head this way and that. He didn't see the need for a lookover when he knew there were no bruises on him. The worst part of his features, he'd say, was probably his eyes. He hadn't been getting the best sleep lately, and didn't doubt there were bags under his eyes.

The man ran his fingers over Ling's ribcage area. Ling resisted the urge to kick him.

"And his ribs aren't popping out of him. He's not skin and bones, unlike the last one you brought in."

"The last one was a rare fluke. This one will bring in the big bucks."

"We'll see." The man took the gag out of Ling's mouth. He wasted no time taking in deep, proper breaths. "What's your name, kid?"

After catching his breath, Ling said, "Why?"

He almost didn't recognize his voice it was so hoarse.

"You don't get to ask questions. What's your name?"

"Ling."

"How old are you?"

"Fifteen."

"Oh, yeah. A young worker will be a good selling point."

Ling really didn't appreciate how they were talking like this in front of him, now that he was paying proper attention and actually being addressed personally. It was different when it was background noise.

"Can you untie my hands and feet? They're sore."

His original captor laughed. "We aren't stupid, kid. We know you'll try to escape as soon as we let you loose."

It would take a while for Ling to be able to remain standing actually. Sore feet aside, he hadn't moved in six days. He'd need some assistance walking and he knew he wouldn't get any. If he tried crawling his way out of here, he'd suffer some kind of punishment, he was sure.

The other man rubbed his chin in deep thought. "This is what we're going to do; we're going to get you some water and food and then we're going to get you set up for the auction. Do you know what that means?"

"Yes. I'm going to be sold to the highest bidder."

"Not your first rodeo?"

Ling shook his head. No point in lying.

"Good, then you know how to behave."

The man stood and wiped his hands off on his pants as if Ling somehow made him dirty. Jerk. He was relieved that he was going to get some food and water soon. That was the only upside to this situation.

His original captor and the new man turned and left Ling alone in his cell.

Until he was let out, Ling didn't know what to do except wait. Again.

Chapter 4: Night of Understanding

Chapter Text

If Edward was immortal, he'd jump off a cliff. It'd relieve some of the pain and be a good distraction. He wondered if Ling ever did that. Die, so he can ignore the world and what he's feeling for a few hours until he was revived.

Currently, Edward was in Mustang's (and technically his) house. It was 1:20AM. Mustang returned back from the office three hours ago. The former Colonel was still awake, had his laptop, desktop, and TV on to keep track of any news regarding Ling. Edward may have had issues with Roy Mustang (the team said it was because he was a 'moody teenager'), but he appreciated that he was going out of his way for Ling.

He wondered if it was only because Ling was Edward's best friend, or if Mustang genuinely cared about him too. Ling was also a kid in his eyes just like Edward was. That could be another reason. It was hard to tell with Mustang. Edward found that frustrating. Whenever he asked, Mustang danced around the topic, so Edward left him alone about it.

Edward was laying on the couch in the living room, staring at his cellphone, searching through random websites.

Tomorrow will be day seven of Ling's disappearance.

What was happening to him right now? Did the person who took him find out he was immortal and was now experimenting on him, like the doctors from three years ago? The idea made Edward's stomach churn. He didn't want to imagine all the different ways Ling might be getting tortured and killed right now. He remembered Ling said one of the doctors from before kept setting him on fire and burning him to death. Edward hoped that wasn't happening right now.

Edward ran a hand over his eyes, putting his phone on his chest. He wanted news about Ling as soon as possible, but he'd be lying if he said staring at screens constantly wasn't messing with his eyes. Even when he did desk work at the office he didn't usually stare at screens too long. He always allowed himself a break.

With Ling missing, Edward couldn't bring himself to do that.

Mustang, who had been sitting in the nearby kitchen, seemed to sense this. Of course he did.

"Why don't you go to sleep, Edward? It's late and you have no excuse not to, unlike the last time I told you to go to rest."

"I don't wanna."

In that moment, Edward didn't care how childish he sounded. He was fifteen, let him have one whiny moment.

"If I get any information on Ling-"

"You'll let me know right away. I know." Edward rolled his eyes. "I wanna be up when it happens."

Despite the space between them, Edward could feel Mustang's annoyance. He didn't care how stubborn he was being. He didn't care that he was a kid and burning out was going to make him useless. He owed it to Ling to be awake.

"I'm also trying to look out for you too, Edward." Mustang's voice was uncharacteristically soft.

Damn it. Of course Mustang would throw that at him. And of course he had to use a soft tone. Edward sighed and rolled onto his side, not moving from the couch, phone held loosely in his hand.

"Yeah, yeah. Well, you're still up."

"It's my job."

"And it's my job as Ling's best friend."

He didn't want to deal with the nightmares too, but Mustang didn't need to know about any of that. Maybe he already knew and decided not to bug him about it. Good.

"What's the first thing you're gonna do when he's back?" Mustang asked.

Edward thought about it for a moment. There were a lot of things he wanted to do when Ling got back. Apologize. Scold him. Hug him. Tell him not to disappear like that again because it's worse than watching him die. Tell him not to scare him again.

"I don't know yet. I just want to see him again. Guess that's the first thing," Edward finally answered. "What are you going to do? I know he isn't under your care the way I am but…"

"If he was under my care, I'd ground him for five years," Mustang replied, dryly.

To Edward, it sounded like how a parent would react. How Mustang reacted to Edward's reckless behaviour sometimes. Those were words neither of them would ever say out loud.

"But he isn't under my care. He still works for me, though, so I'm going to take him off duty for a while. No more going out on the field with you."

Edward scowled. "But he's my partner. I need him with me when I go out."

"Then I guess you're going to be stuck inside with him."

Edward groaned.

As much as he hated to admit it, talking with Mustang helped a little. It didn't make his worry or anxiety for Ling go away completely, if at all, but it was nice to talk about him as if he'll walk in the door any minute now. Come in, alive and well, and say, "I'm back! I know it's late but what's for dinner?"

They both knew that wasn't going to happen.

Silence followed after that. The only real sound was that of the news channel on the TV and even then the volume was low. Edward didn't think there was going to be anymore conversation until morning or afternoon or whenever he decided to talk to Mustang. He started to feel himself fall asleep when Mustang spoke up.

"I had a fight with Hughes the night he died."

Edward sat up, head spinning from the abrupt action. He turned to face Mustang. The look on the older man's face was pensive. This was the last thing Edward expected tonight. Mustang almost never talked about his past. He barely got Mustang's history with Riza Hawkeye. A story of love that didn't get the chance to progress before things came crashing down. Edward had to hear it from the other members of the unit first.

Watching him, Edward noticed that Mustang didn't seem like the thirty-something year old he was. He looked more so like a tired old man.

"It still eats away at me," Mustang continued. There was sympathy in his eyes that Edward didn't think he's seen from the man before. "I know what you're going through."

Edward worried his lip, casting his eyes downward. What was he supposed to say to that? What was the appropriate thing? He wasn't a very touchy-feely person. Those moments were few and far between and especially when it came to Mustang.

Maybe Mustang didn't even mean to say any of this. Maybe he was delirious from being awake so late and from working so hard. He had been putting in extra work ever since Ling disappeared. He probably wouldn't remember this conversation after he got some sleep. Edward would, though.

"I'm sorry."

It was all Edward could think of to say.

They didn't speak for the rest of the night. Edward fell asleep and had nightmares of Ling, Alphonse, and Mustang. They were all suffering in various ways and Edward could do nothing to stop it.
