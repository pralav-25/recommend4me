True Self Blooms

Ideality

Summary:

Last year was, to put it mildly, a bit of a clusterfuck. The good news is this year sort of has to be better, at least on the education front. No one really questions how things ended, which is good for the people involved, who very much did get away with murder.

There's a lot going on behind the scenes, but it's not really most students' problem. Even as the world burns down around them, they're focused on continuing their education. Most of them. For those who know the truth of last year, though, there is one question that needs to be asked.

Just what sort of person is Ling Yao, really?

(Or: Idiot homunculus faces the consequences of a terrible upbringing.)

Chapter 1: Planting Seeds

Notes:

(See the end of the chapter for notes.)

Chapter Text

Luna Lovegood presented a pile of papers to her father with as wide a grin as she could muster. “What do you think?”

“This is very well written,” he admitted, and she allowed herself a flicker of satisfaction. “This is all true?”

“The people I spoke to are all willing to put their names behind it. They even understood that I couldn’t publish it right away.” Now that she’d handed over the papers, it was time to find something to eat. A sandwich, maybe? That sounded nice and simple. “I think it’s important for people to know.”

There were people who wouldn’t think the same way. The British Ministry of Magic, for one. Nothing that was written there was very positive about them. But if they had a problem with that, they shouldn’t have let people do horrible things in their name.

Her father put the papers down with a sigh. “Why didn’t you tell me it was still like that?”

“It wasn’t something you could change from the outside.” The residents of Hogsmeade had tried. They’d seen the students, with their tired eyes and scarred hands, and done whatever they could to help. “I didn’t want to make you feel bad without giving you a way to help.” It was like in her first year, when others were taking her things. He couldn’t do anything about that, so there was no point in making him worry.

And- and. Between the two of them, Luna was the one with credibility. That said something she was choosing not to examine.

The world was already on fire. No point doing anything but fanning the flames in the right direction. She could dwell on the results later. With her friends. And think about how to keep changing the world the way they wanted.

Maybe with less murder. She hadn’t been told outright that had happened, but she could guess. It wasn’t hard.

If it was all the same, she’d stick with more intellectual means of revolution.

 

It had taken time, and effort, and a lot of complaining at sympathetic portraits, but Albus Dumbledore had finally managed to get his paperwork backlog looking like it wasn’t about to come to life and start eating people. Half of it came from sorting out things that no longer applied and having Fawkes set them on fire. The rest was just sitting down and working for multiple days straight.

He really was getting too old for this.

If only the backlog wouldn’t keep increasing. If only people would stop sending him things. But new work just kept coming in, because that was what happened when doing something with as much administrative effort involved as running a school.

Hogwarts was usually worth it. It was a fine building with fine students and a fine legacy going back a thousand years. But the paperwork.

Some of it would take multiple days to get through. Changes to centuries of tradition, wrapped in more bureaucracy than anyone in those days would have been able to anticipate. More than anyone who saw the modern form would ever have expected.

To say nothing of the people coming in and out of the Chamber of Secrets this year. The sooner an alternative means of access for that was figured out, the better, but for now, all of that still fell to him.

And there was also complaints from parents that technically didn’t apply anymore, but would still be a good idea to read for political purposes.

...Why did he want this job, again?

A new note landed on Albus desk. It looked short and easy to deal with. He picked it up.

We’re tired of hiding. We want people to know who we are. Make it happen. -GreedLing Yao

...Well, there wasn’t a lot he could do about that, other than have another headache. It was at least a novel request.

The sender of the note was very familiar to Albus. Greed the Avaricious was a homunculus housed within the body of Ling Yao, who happened to be one of his students. It wasn’t a situation he’d ever envisioned before they explained it to him. They seemed happy enough, though, and he had other things to worry about, like Tom Riddle and his merry band of troublemakers, so he let it be. The truth of their situation was already circulating around the school, but apparently they wanted him to speed it up somehow.

He made a note for Minerva and Severus to officially add Greed’s name to the student roster. That was, in fact, the most he could do about them. But it was enough that he could say he’d kept them from adding to his backlog, and that would have to be good enough for now.

There were things he had to do, now that the pile was smaller and unlikely to crush anyone under its weight. And also things he needed to do in general.

It was time to talk to Severus.

 

In most situations, Albus would never have hired Severus Snape on as a teacher. The man had neither aptitude nor enthusiasm for the job. But Horace had been looking to retire at the time, and it had seemed convenient for keeping him close by.

Now that there were fewer circumstances locking him into that choice, Albus could admit that had probably been a mistake.

Oh, the scores on the standardized tests were incredible. But making children hate one of their core subjects in the process was never going to be a good idea.

Whether the current move was any better… only time would tell. “As you may recall, the post of Defense Against the Dark Arts professor is currently vacant.” It was the tactful way to put it. The last holder of the post, one Dolores Umbridge, had met her end in a very painful and messy fashion. The position had been cursed, and a likely physical anchor for that curse had been located by a certain pair of students. She stole the anchor from them and placed it directly on her head.

All loss of life was, in some way, a tragedy. In this case, most of that tragedy came from the fact that several uninvolved students had been there to witness it.

More importantly, the curse was likely gone. Severus sighed. “Does this mean you’ve finally come around?”

“Yes, you can have the job.” After multiple years of not being able to find other applicants, qualified or not, Albus had to admit defeat. Even if the curse wasn’t gone, it would at least be a year where he could undergo a completely different set of job interviews. That would be nice. Change things up a little. “Would you like me to take this time to inform you of various changes that will be implemented this year, or do you just want to wait for the notice the same as everyone else?”

“I can wait.” The look on his face very clearly said ‘get out of my house,’ but Albus chose to ignore it.

There were other worries to deal with.

“In that case, there’s just one more thing.” He just needed to get this over with, and then he could go back to his nice office that was covered in paperwork and keep whittling away at it. Maybe with some cake. Cake would be nice. “I have a certain errand to run that I would like your assistance with.”

Albus would never be able to decide if extending that invitation was a good or bad idea. He suspected it would have ended the same way no matter what.

Either way, it turned out that the piece of paperwork he would most need to be concerned with in the future was writing his will.
